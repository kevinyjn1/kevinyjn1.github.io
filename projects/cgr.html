CGR: Conversational Graph Retrieval

Long-horizon conversations — sessions spanning days or weeks — break standard RAG systems because flat vector search loses track of why something was said. CGR encodes conversation history as a typed knowledge graph and retrieves relevant memories using intent-aware graph traversal rather than cosine similarity alone.

Motivation

The LoCoMo benchmark was designed to stress-test long-term conversational memory. The MAGMA baseline (which uses a memory graph) performs well on recent turns but degrades on references that require chaining multiple past events. The hypothesis: a retrieval step that respects conversational intent — not just semantic similarity — should do better.

Approach

Each conversation turn is parsed into entities, events, and relations and inserted into a graph. At query time, an intent classifier routes the query to a subgraph traversal strategy: recency-weighted walk for follow-up questions, entity-centric expansion for questions about a specific person or object, and semantic fallback for novel topics. Retrieved subgraphs are linearized and passed to the LLM as context.

Results

Benchmarked against MAGMA on LoCoMo. CGR improves recall on multi-hop questions while remaining competitive on single-hop factual lookups. Full results to be written up.

Stack

Python, NetworkX, sentence-transformers, GPT-4o, LoCoMo benchmark.
