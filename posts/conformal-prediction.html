Why surgical AI needs to know when it's wrong

Most machine learning models are trained to be accurate. Fewer are trained to be honest about how accurate they are on any given input. For a model that recommends a movie, that gap doesn't matter much. For a model tracking a surgeon's hands during an operation, it matters enormously.

The problem with a single number

A typical pose estimator outputs a best-guess coordinate for each joint. Maybe it's right to within a millimeter; maybe it's off by a centimeter because the instrument occluded the hand for three frames. The output looks identical either way — just coordinates. The downstream system has no principled way to tell the confident predictions from the shaky ones.

A confident wrong answer and an uncertain wrong answer score the same on most benchmarks. In an OR they are not remotely the same.

What conformal prediction buys you

Conformal prediction is a framework for turning any model's outputs into prediction sets (or intervals) that come with a finite-sample coverage guarantee. Instead of "the joint is here," you get "the joint is within this region, and that region contains the true location 90% of the time."

The split-conformal version is simple enough to describe in a sentence: hold out a calibration set, measure how wrong the model tends to be on it, and use that empirical error distribution to size the prediction region for new inputs. No retraining, no distributional assumptions about the model. 

calibration_scores = |y_true - y_pred|   # on held-out data
q = quantile(calibration_scores, 0.90)   # the 90% threshold
interval = y_pred ± q                     # guaranteed coverage

The piece I'm working on is making this per-joint and temporal — different fingers have different uncertainty, and that uncertainty changes frame to frame as the hand moves in and out of view. A wide interval becomes a useful signal: it's the model raising its hand to say it isn't sure.

Why it's the right primitive for safety

Once uncertainty is calibrated, you can build on it: gate a downstream action when the interval is too wide, flag frames for human review, or weight a temporal model toward the confident observations. The guarantee is what makes it trustworthy rather than just another heuristic.

That's the thread running through most of what I'm building this summer. More soon.
